# Survey About Video and Multimodal Generation

## TL;DR
- Video generation has evolved significantly with frameworks such as VAE-GAN, outperforming older models in diverse applications like text-to-video generation [1]. 
- Recent architectures focus on efficiency and quality, with innovations like diffusion models enabling high-fidelity outputs [2]. 
- Benchmarks such as TC-Bench reveal gaps in model performance regarding temporal compositionality in generated videos, highlighting areas for future research [3].

## Background
Video generation encompasses diverse techniques employed to synthesize dynamic content from static inputs, closely tied to advancements in machine learning methods. Foundational works have proposed various models including GANs (Generative Adversarial Networks) and variational methods that serve as the backbone for contemporary approaches.[1]

## Theme 1: Foundations and Theoretical Formulations
At its core, video generation combines elements from both computer vision and machine learning, focusing on understanding spatial and temporal dynamics within multimedia contexts. Recent studies emphasize mental modeling in multi-agent systems for complex decision-making scenarios [3]. This conceptual framework aids in addressing the intricacies of how agents perceive and interact within visual environments, vital for improving multimodal applications. 

## Theme 2: Core Architectures and Model Taxonomy
Innovative architectures such as the hybrid VAE-GAN framework have revolutionized video generation by improving diversity and fidelity through effective training of GAN encounters [2]. Furthermore, recent contributions highlight long-video storytelling techniques that incorporate narrative consistency while employing advanced architectures for detailed character-driven outputs, thus enriching user experiences. 

## Theme 3: Empirical Benchmarks and Real-World Applications
Evaluations conducted through frameworks like TC-Bench illustrate significant deficiencies in existing models concerning temporal compositionality in video generation [3]. Such benchmarks pave the way for establishing standards in evaluating model performance, fostering competition and growth in the field of multimodal generation. 

## Trends and open problems
The domain faces lingering challenges, including the need for improved robustness in generating coherent sequences across various modalities. Current research still grapples with issues such as repetitive output patterns and the integration of multiple input types into cohesive outputs, posing significant hurdles for broad adoption in real-world applications. New methodologies are essential to refine these processes, expanding the potential use cases for video generation technologies
