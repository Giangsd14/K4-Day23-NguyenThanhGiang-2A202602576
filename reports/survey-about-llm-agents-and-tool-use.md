# Survey about LLM Agents and Tool Use

## TL;DR
- LLM agents introduce unique challenges in security and operational risks related to tool use and external content sources [1].
- Architectural approaches in LLMs dictate their autonomy and decision-making capabilities, influencing their deployment in real-world scenarios [2].
- Recent tests reveal crucial disparities in performance metrics among various LLM models, indicating the necessity for standardized benchmarks [3].

## Background
The advent of LLM agents has broadened the horizons for automated systems capable of complex decision-making and interactions. The foundational work surrounding these agents has established critical definitions and taxonomies which inform their application and theoretical basis [4]. 

## Foundations and Theoretical Formulations
Various studies have explored the security concerns associated with LLM agents, establishing frameworks for understanding potential failure modes and risk factors [1]. For instance, the work on agentic AI highlights new vulnerabilities that arise from the capabilities of persistent memory and external tool usage [4]. Additionally, a taxonomy for agentic recommender systems is explored, providing insight into the autonomy levels and interaction paradigms available to LLM systems [1].

## Core Architectures and Model Taxonomy
The architectural designs underpinning LLM agents significantly impact their functions and efficiencies [2]. A notable framework, the LLM-Agent-UMF, proposes clear definitions for agent composition, enhancing the modularity of agent systems [2]. Moreover, advancements in autonomous decision-making have clarified how LLMs can utilize tools effectively, a critical insight for future developments [2].

## Empirical Benchmarks and Real-World Applications
Emerging benchmarks have identified considerable performance variations within LLM frameworks, emphasizing the need for better-defined evaluation criteria [3]. For instance, the empirical study on recursive self-improvement methodologies introduces benchmarks that guide agent behavior in real-world applications [3]. These insights underline the importance of optimized testing environments to improve validation processes in LLM deployments [2].

## Trends and Open Problems
The current landscape of LLM agents faces numerous challenges including security threats, operational effectiveness, and the integration of evolving technologies [5]. Understanding these difficulties is paramount as the field evolves, with ongoing research directed toward addressing these gaps and enhancing LLM autonomy through better architectural decisions [4].
